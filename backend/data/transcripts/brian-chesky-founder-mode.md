# Brian Chesky on Founder Mode, Leading Airbnb, and Building Design-Driven Products

**Episode:** 122  
**Guest:** Brian Chesky (Co-founder & CEO of Airbnb)  
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky  

---

(00:01:20) **Lenny Rachitsky:** Brian Chesky, welcome to the show. You recently gave a talk at Y Combinator that took Silicon Valley by storm, talking about "Founder Mode." Can you explain what Founder Mode is and why the conventional advice given to scaling founders was broken?

(00:02:45) **Brian Chesky:** For decades, the conventional business school and Silicon Valley wisdom was: *"Hire professional managers, delegate everything, step back, and let people run their divisions autonomously."*
We tried that at Airbnb as we scaled, and it almost destroyed the company. What happens when you delegate completely to dozens of autonomous divisions is that everyone optimizes for their own local KPI, not the holistic customer experience. You end up with 10 different products that feel disjointed, feature bloat, and bureaucracy.
When COVID hit in 2020 and our revenue dropped by 80% in eight weeks, I had to take back control.
**Founder Mode** means:
1. Staying in the details of the product.
2. Reviewing every single major feature before it ships.
3. Operating the company as one unified roadmap rather than a federation of disconnected teams.
4. Elevating design and taste to equal footing with engineering and finance.

(00:10:15) **Lenny Rachitsky:** How did you restructure product management at Airbnb?

(00:11:00) **Brian Chesky:** We got rid of the traditional product management function as people know it.
In many companies, PMs become "ticket movers" or project managers who write specifications that designers and engineers execute without excitement.
At Airbnb, we combined product management with product marketing. Our PMs must understand positioning, customer storytelling, and distribution just as much as feature scoping.
Furthermore, we run a unified, twice-a-year release cycle: our Summer Release and Winter Release. Every team ships together. This forces cross-functional alignment and creates massive, coordinated PR and marketing moments.

(00:18:30) **Lenny Rachitsky:** What is your advice on customer obsession?

(00:19:15) **Brian Chesky:** Most companies do quantitative research on thousands of users and look at spreadsheets. But spreadsheets don't have emotion.
In the early days of Airbnb, Joe, Nate, and I literally lived in our hosts' homes in New York City. We knocked on doors, took photos of their apartments, had breakfast with them, and wrote code based on what they said at the kitchen table.
If you can make 100 people love your product passionately, they will tell everyone they know. It is much better to have 100 people who love you than 100,000 people who just kind of like you.
