# Elena Verna on B2B Product-Led Growth, Product-Led Sales, and Freemium Loops

**Episode:** 68  
**Guest:** Elena Verna (Head of Growth at Dropbox, SurveyMonkey, Miro, Netlify, Lovable)  
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky  

---

(00:01:15) **Lenny Rachitsky:** Welcome to the podcast. Today I'm joined by Elena Verna, one of the most respected growth advisors in tech. Elena, you've worked with Miro, Dropbox, SurveyMonkey, Netlify, and many others. To kick things off, what is the single biggest misconception founders and PMs have about Product-Led Growth (PLG) in B2B?

(00:02:40) **Elena Verna:** The biggest misconception is thinking PLG is just a self-serve checkout page or a free trial button. People treat PLG as a pricing tactic or a marketing channel. It is not. Product-Led Growth is a company-wide distribution model where the product itself drives customer acquisition, retention, and monetization.
In traditional sales-led growth, you convince a buyer before they ever touch the software. In product-led growth, the end-user falls in love with the product first, experiences the "aha moment" immediately, and then pulls the product into the organization.

(00:07:30) **Lenny Rachitsky:** Let's break down the debate between Freemium and Free Trial. How do you advise teams to decide between the two?

(00:08:15) **Elena Verna:** This is one of my favorite topics. Here is the rule of thumb:
1. **Free Trial** works when your product has high urgency, clear immediate value, and a time-bound evaluation window. It puts a clock on the user. If they don't buy in 14 or 30 days, access is cut off.
2. **Freemium** works when value accumulates over time, or when your product has strong viral or collaborative network effects—like Miro, Figma, or Slack. If you shut down access in 14 days, you kill the viral loop before their teammates can join.
If your product requires collaboration, freemium is almost always mandatory. Freemium creates a top-of-funnel wedge. But you must design the paywall around "feature gates" or "usage limits" (like number of boards or storage), never gating the core value proposition.

(00:14:20) **Lenny Rachitsky:** How does Product-Led Sales (PLS) fit into this? When should a team layer sales on top of a self-serve funnel?

(00:15:05) **Elena Verna:** PLS is the natural evolution of PLG. You don't replace self-serve with sales; sales harvests the demand created by self-serve.
The mistake companies make is hiring enterprise sales reps who start cold-outreaching prospects with zero product usage. Instead, your sales team should look at **Product Qualified Leads (PQLs)**.
A PQL is an account that has already crossed your activation milestone—for instance, 5 active users collaborating on 3 different projects inside a corporate domain. When a sales rep reaches out to a PQL, it's not a cold pitch. It's: *"Hey, we noticed 8 people on your team are using our tool. Would you like enterprise security, SAML SSO, and centralized billing?"* That conversation closes at a 3x higher conversion rate than traditional outbound.

(00:22:10) **Lenny Rachitsky:** What about retention? What are the retention loops that actually move the needle?

(00:23:00) **Elena Verna:** In B2B, the best retention loop is the **collaborative loop**. When Person A creates an artifact (a dashboard, a board, a document) and shares it with Person B to review, Person B is forced to become an active user to complete their job.
The second loop is the **habit-building notification loop**. If your product integrates into where the team already lives—Slack, email, or GitHub—you pull users back into the core loop without paying Google or Meta for re-acquisition.
Always remember: Growth without retention is just pouring water into a leaky bucket.
