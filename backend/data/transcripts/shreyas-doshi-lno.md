# Shreyas Doshi on The LNO Framework, High-Agency Product Leadership, and Career Strategy

**Episode:** 45  
**Guest:** Shreyas Doshi (Product Leader at Stripe, Twitter, Google, Yahoo)  
**Host:** Lenny Rachitsky  

---

(00:01:05) **Lenny Rachitsky:** Welcome, Shreyas Doshi. Shreyas, you've developed some of the most widely shared frameworks in product management. One framework everyone in tech asks about is the **LNO Framework**. Could you unpack what LNO stands for and how PMs should use it?

(00:02:15) **Shreyas Doshi:** The LNO Framework is a prioritization system for high-performing product managers and leaders.
The fundamental dilemma of knowledge workers, especially PMs, is that your work volume is infinite. If you treat every task with the same standard of perfection, you will inevitably burn out and underperform on the things that actually matter.
LNO categorizes all tasks into three distinct buckets:
1. **L = Leverage Tasks:** These are tasks where doing an exceptional, 10x job creates disproportionate, non-linear upside for your company or team. Examples include defining the multi-year product strategy, designing the core onboarding flow for millions of users, or hiring a key engineering lead. For Leverage tasks, you give 100% of your energy, focus, and craft.
2. **N = Neutral Tasks:** These are tasks where doing a good, competent job is necessary, but doing a 10x job creates minimal additional value. Examples include regular weekly status reports, routine PR reviews, and standard team operational meetings. For Neutral tasks, aim for an 80% standard and execute with speed.
3. **O = Overhead Tasks:** These are tasks where simply completing them or getting them off your plate is the objective. Examples include routine expense reports, administrative scheduling, or compliance checkboxes. For Overhead tasks, do a "just good enough" job (say 50-60% perfection) or delegate/automate them completely.

(00:08:45) **Lenny Rachitsky:** How does a PM actually audit their week using LNO?

(00:09:30) **Shreyas Doshi:** Take your calendar and todo list for the past two weeks. Color-code every block: Green for Leverage, Yellow for Neutral, Red for Overhead.
Most struggling PMs find that 70% of their time is consumed by Overhead and Neutral tasks, leaving only 10-20% for Leverage. High-agency PMs ruthlessly protect at least 30-40% of their calendar for deep, uninterrupted Leverage work.

(00:15:20) **Lenny Rachitsky:** Another concept you talk about is "High Agency". What does high agency mean in practice?

(00:16:10) **Shreyas Doshi:** High agency is the refusal to accept standard constraints as fixed. When a low-agency person encounters a blocker—like *"Legal said no"* or *"Engineering doesn't have bandwidth this quarter"*—they stop and say *"I did what I could."*
A high-agency person bends reality. They ask: *"Why did Legal say no? What specific risk are they protecting against? Can we adjust the scope to mitigate that risk while preserving 90% of user value?"* High agency is having the relentless determination to find an ethical, creative path forward when ordinary people give up.

(00:23:40) **Lenny Rachitsky:** What about pre-mortems? Why are pre-mortems so critical for product launches?

(00:24:25) **Shreyas Doshi:** A post-mortem happens after your product has failed. The patient is already dead.
A **pre-mortem** happens before you launch. You gather your entire cross-functional team—engineering, design, product, sales, support—and say: *"Imagine it is one year from today, and this launch was an utter catastrophe. What caused it?"*
This psychological reframing gives permission to skeptics and quiet engineers to speak openly without sounding negative. You catch blind spots when they are cheap to fix, rather than learning them in production.
